You climb over the largest section carefully, aware that loose stones here could shift without warning.
Cracked pottery shards litter the floor around brick ovens in a buried kiln complex that once burned hotter than any kiln should reasonably need, though it looks as undisturbed as everything nearby. Ash has settled into drifts that no one has ever swept away across the entire chamber, though whatever kept it that way is long gone. You lift one shard carefully, studying the glaze for any hint of what it was originally meant to hold.
Rotted robes hang from hooks along the wall of a sunken vestry, the fabric reduced to little more than colored threads by centuries of damp. Chests beneath the robes still hold ceremonial items tarnished past recognition by the dark they have sat in so long, though it hardly seems worth a second thought now. You open the nearest chest slowly, uncertain whether disturbing it counts as trespass against something once sacred.
Sagging floors hold the weight of grain turned to dust long ago in a granary loft that should have collapsed long before you arrived, and none of it explains why the air still feels disturbed. Support beams below have splintered in places, and the floor creaks with every step you take across it. You test each board before committing your weight, aware the whole loft could give way at any moment.
Rusted valves mark the junctions of an aqueduct system that once split water to serve every quarter of the fortress above, though the reason for it died with whoever built this. Most valves have fused into a single fixed position, sending what little flow remains toward a forgotten wing. You trace the driest channel back to a source that ran dry longer ago than anyone thought to record, the kind of quiet that outlasts whoever left it behind.
Dissolved labels leave nothing to identify the contents of jars lining the shelves of a flooded larder vault, though you get the sense it always looked this way. What remains inside has reduced to a dark residue darker than anything a jar should reasonably hold, though nothing suggests anyone is coming back for it. You leave the jars undisturbed, unwilling to learn what an eternity underwater has done to whatever they once held.
Rotted railings guard a choir loft overlooking a nave far below, its wooden benches still arranged as though a service might resume any moment. Sheet music left on the stands has fused into blocks whose notation no longer means anything to anyone who might try to read it, a fact the stone itself seems content to keep. You lean carefully over the railing, testing its strength before trusting it with your weight.
Roofed over by centuries of collapsed stone, an open air tanning yard hardens beneath a crust thick enough to walk across without sinking. Residue in the pits has darkened over time into something closer to solid ground than to the liquid it once was, though the reason for that has long since faded. You cross the hardened pits carefully, watching for any section softer than the rest.
Iron rings still bolt to the walls of a sunken stable block green with age no living hand has scrubbed away, and no mark on the stone suggests otherwise. Compressed straw crunches oddly underfoot, the last sound this chamber seems to remember how to make on its own. You pick your way between the stalls, listening for anything that might still be moving somewhere in the dark.
Scattered crockery is all that remains on the stripped shelves of a collapsed pantry cellar otherwise emptied by time, every shelf bracket still bolted firmly to the wall. Dust coats every surface thick enough to hold the impression of footsteps that crossed it long before you arrived, the prints heading toward a door that no longer opens. You follow the old footprints, curious where they lead and who might have made them.
Snapped threads unravel across the empty frames of a buried loom house sealed away generations before anyone thought to finish the weave, shuttle after shuttle left exactly where the last hand set it down. Bobbins scattered across the floor still hold traces of dye in colors no longer produced anywhere above, one spindle still wound tight enough to unspool at a touch. You gather one bobbin, turning it over to study a color you cannot name in a place that no longer remembers names.
Fused tallow candles hold the shape of a shelf that collapsed around them decades before anyone noticed the wax had spread this far, and no mark on the stone suggests otherwise. Wicks still protrude from the fused block in neat rows, the last visible trace of the shelving that once held them upright. You break off the smallest piece you can manage, curious whether it would still burn if given a flame.
Rusted hoops crumble to dust beside bundled barrel staves inside a collapsed cooperage shed abandoned partway through the work, the staves themselves still bound in tidy stacked bundles. A half-finished barrel sits on the workbench nearby, its final hoop never fitted before the workshop was sealed for good. You lift the unfinished barrel and find it lighter than expected, as though the wood itself has hollowed with age.
