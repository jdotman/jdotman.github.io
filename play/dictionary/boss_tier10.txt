Boots and coiled whips hang from pegs above each cot, left behind by men who clearly expected a far shorter stay than this. You sit briefly on the nearest cot and feel the whole frame collapse beneath even your carefully measured weight.
Cards fan face down across a round table at the center of a sealed fortune teller's parlor, undisturbed since whatever reading was left unfinished there. Candles long since burned to stubs ring the table in a pattern that once meant something to whoever lit them. You turn the topmost card over and find its painted face worn past recognition, whatever fortune it held gone with everyone who might have read it.
Bottles rest in long racks throughout a vintner's cellar, their labels dissolved into an unreadable film by centuries of persistent damp air. Corks inside the oldest bottles have crumbled steadily inward, leaving the wine within exposed to air that turned it fully to vinegar long ago. You lift one bottle carefully and hold it to the light, unable to tell whether it once held a vintage genuinely worth remembering.
Chairs face a raised platform at the center of a sealed mourning hall, arranged as though a viewing might still resume there at any moment. Dark drapery along the walls has faded to a color that no longer has a proper name, brittle enough to crumble at the lightest touch. You approach the empty platform slowly, uncertain whether the silence here asks for grief or simply permits it.
A punt with a flat bottom sits half sunk at a flooded ferryman's dock, its mooring rope still looped tight around a post no boat will ever leave again. Coins meant as toll lie scattered along the stone landing, left by hands that stopped counting them long ago. You test the punt's hull with your boot and feel the wood give slightly, soft with a rot that has had all the time it needed.
Wooden lasts of every size line the shelves of a collapsed cobbler's shed, each one still shaped for a foot that stopped needing shoes long ago. Boots finished only halfway sit clamped to benches nearby, stitching abandoned partway around the sole. You lift the nearest last and turn it over, wondering whose foot it was ever meant to fit.
Barrels and crates fill the storeroom of an inn that stopped serving travelers longer ago than travel itself seems to have existed at this depth. Tankards stacked along one wall have tarnished well past recognition, though a few still hold the dried ring of whatever they last actually contained. You lift the nearest tankard and find it far lighter than expected, hollowed out by rust as much as by time itself.
Pots and pans in every conceivable stage of disrepair fill the stall of a tinker who never quite finished the last order he ever took. Solder and small tools sit exactly where he set them down, arranged for a repair that centuries have since made clear will never actually resume. You lift the nearest pot carefully and find the patch on its base only halfway soldered into place.
Fused into a single solid mass, the tallow candles of a sunken chandlery cellar still show wicks protruding in rows from what was once careful shelving. The block has held its shape under a pressure that built slowly for longer than pressure itself has had a name here, though the dark keeps whatever else there is to know. You break off the smallest piece you can manage, wondering whether flame still means anything at this depth.
Half formed nails sit cooling forever inside the trough of a nailmaker's forge that lost its fire many generations ago. Bins along the wall hold finished nails rusted solid into a single mass far too heavy for any hand to separate now. You pry one nail free from the fused bin and find it simply bends rather than holding any point at all.
Untended hides sit stacked in a tannery vault where workers vanished before the curing process could ever be completed, though the reason for that has long since faded. Salt has crystallized across every surface, coating the stacked hides in a crust that crumbles at the gentlest touch. You brush the crust from the nearest hide, curious whether anything beneath it survived this depth at all.
Marks left by abandoned tools line blocks cut only halfway through a sunken quarry pit where the work simply stopped one day and never resumed, and the walls hold whatever answer once existed. Chisels and mallets lie fused together where they were dropped, rusted into a shape too old for any tool to reasonably claim as its own. You study the unfinished cuts one last time, wondering whether anything left this deep was ever meant to be completed.
