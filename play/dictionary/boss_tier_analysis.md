# Boss Tier Passage Analysis

Analysis of `public/dictionary/boss_tier1.txt` through `boss_tier10.txt`, generated by measuring every line (passage) and every sentence within it. Use this as the spec for writing new tiers or recreating existing ones with the same shape.

## Structure

- Every file has exactly **100 lines** (passages), one passage per line.
- Sentence count per passage is **fixed within a tier** and increases in lockstep every 2 tiers:
  - Tiers 1–2: **1 sentence** per passage
  - Tiers 3–6: **2 sentences** per passage
  - Tiers 7–10: **3 sentences** per passage
- Sentences are split on `.`/`!`/`?` followed by whitespace (standard sentence boundary).
- **Comma rule (tier 5 and above):** commas are used only where a sentence genuinely calls for one — a true independent clause joined by "and" (different subject on each side) or a fronted phrase before the main clause — never inserted to hit a quota, and never for a merely stylistic pause (e.g. a causal "as" clause reads fine unpunctuated, so that comma was dropped). Comma frequency rises naturally with tier (more clauses per sentence → more genuine comma-worthy constructions), not by mandate: tier 5 ~1.1/line, tier 6 ~1.7/line, tier 7 ~2.3/line, tier 8 ~2.2/line, tier 9 ~2.0/line, tier 10 ~2.9/line (tier 10 also legitimately uses multi-comma sentences — parentheticals like "somehow," and tricolons like "this deep, this final, this alone" — where every comma in the sentence is structurally necessary, not decorative; the still-pruned pattern is the flat coordinate-adjective pair, e.g. "dark, cracked crust"). Tiers 1–4 stay comma-free. Tiers 7 and 8 were rewritten (see below) to remove a shared pool of stock ending-tag phrases, which pushed both tiers' comma-per-line rate above tier 9's — this is fine since comma frequency is descriptive, not the load-bearing progression stat (sentence length is; see next bullet). The target is that a player reaches tier 5 and starts expecting commas to show up regularly, without any sentence being forced to carry one it doesn't need.
- **Hyphen rule (tier 9–10 only):** a hyphen is used only where standard English grammar actually requires one — a genuine attributive compound modifier before a noun (e.g. "half-finished kettle," "war-scarred giant") or a fixed compound like "slack-jawed" or "cross-legged" that's conventionally hyphenated regardless of position — and only starting at tier 9. Never insert a hyphen to hit a quota or for stylistic flourish; most words in tier 9-10 passages have no reason to take one, and that's expected. Tiers 1–8 avoid hyphens entirely, and that means avoiding the underlying construction, not just dropping the mark — don't write "half finished kettle" (grammatically missing its hyphen) as a workaround; rephrase around it instead ("a kettle that was never finished," "a kettle abandoned partway through"). Hyphen handling isn't introduced as a keystroke complexity until the final two tiers, so no tier 1–8 sentence should read as though a hyphen is missing. Do not hyphenate a resultative construction like "worn smooth" (verb + adjective complement, not a compound modifier) at any tier — that's a different grammatical shape and never takes a hyphen.

## Progression pattern (for designing new tiers)

- **Passage char average** climbs roughly linearly within each sentence-count bracket, then jumps at each bracket boundary (1→2→3 sentences):
  - 1-sentence bracket (tiers 1–2): ~65 → ~85 chars/passage
  - 2-sentence bracket (tiers 3–6): ~174 → ~229 chars/passage
  - 3-sentence bracket (tiers 7–10): ~362 → ~415 chars/passage
- **Average sentence length** (chars/sentence) also climbs steadily tier-over-tier, independent of the sentence-count bracket: from ~65 at tier 1 up to ~138 at tier 10. This is the main lever for difficulty within a bracket — later tiers in the same bracket use longer, more complex sentences rather than more of them.
- **Monotonic progression is load-bearing for game balance.** Sentence-length average must strictly increase tier-over-tier (tier N+1 avg > tier N avg) even when a tier is regenerated independently — rewriting one tier in isolation (e.g. to add the comma rule) can easily overshoot or undershoot neighboring tiers if only checked against the old spec numbers rather than against the tiers actually sitting on either side of it. Always re-diff the full avg-sentence-length column across all 10 tiers after touching any one of them.
- **Watch for AI-generation tells when writing ~100 passages back to back.** Bulk generation drifts toward stock phrases reused verbatim (found and fixed: "simply, quietly" used 7x, "the concept of X itself has technically Y" used ~20x, and a "[Buried/Sunken/Collapsed] [noun] complexes preserve..." template opening 46-70 of 100 lines in tiers 8-10). After writing a tier, grep it for repeated 2-4 word phrases and for how many lines share the same opening template; anything appearing more than ~2-3 times verbatim needs manual variation. Padding sentences to hit a length target is itself a common source of this — a trailing clause added only to add characters (not to say something) reads as filler even when grammatically fine, so prefer varying wording over appending a stock tail phrase.
- **This tell also shows up as reuse *across* tiers, not just within one.** Tiers 6 and 7 were independently found to share a pool of only ~16 stock third-sentence tension tags ("shoulders squared against the dark," "grip tight on the worn hilt," etc.), reused 2-4x each per file with the *same pool* shared verbatim between the two tiers — both were rewritten to drop the shared tag pool in favor of organic, per-passage closing beats. Separately, ~40 of tier 8's 60 boss encounters and ~10 of its 40 room descriptions turned out to be lightly reworded paraphrases of tier 7's passages (same creature/room concept, same sentence shape, a word or two swapped); tier 8 was fully rewritten with fresh subjects and verified via a `difflib.SequenceMatcher` pairwise pass against tiers 5-7 and 9-10 (all ratios now ≤0.45). When rewriting one tier, always diff its subjects/phrases against its immediate neighbors, not just check it in isolation.
- **Variance (stdev)** in passage length is tight in early tiers (low single digits to ~8), tight at tier 5 (~7) and tier 6 (~13) since both were rewritten with the judgment-based comma rule (which naturally narrows the length band), then widens considerably from tier 7 onward (~18–26), meaning tiers 7+ mix noticeably shorter and longer passages rather than keeping uniform length.

## Recipe to add a new tier (e.g. tier 11+)

1. Decide the sentence-count bracket by extending the pattern (tiers 11–14 would likely use 4 sentences/passage, following the every-2-tiers-plus-1 progression... but note tiers 1–2 only got 2 tiers at 1 sentence before bumping, so a 4th bracket could start as early as tier 11).
2. Target ~100 passages.
3. Target avg sentence length above the previous tier's average (continue the ~10–15 char/tier climb seen in tiers 7–10), and let individual sentences range roughly ±40–50 chars around that average.
4. Target avg passage length above the previous tier's average, consistent with (sentences/passage × avg sentence length).
5. Keep passage-length stdev in the 20–30 range to match the variance style of tiers 5–10, rather than tightly clustering every passage to the same length.
